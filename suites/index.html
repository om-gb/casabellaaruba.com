The building

One floor, eight doors, no lift and no corridor smell.

Casabella Suites is a single-storey block finished in 2026. Everything is on one level — no stairs to a front door, no lift to wait for, no suitcase hauled up half a flight at midnight.

A shaded walkway runs down the middle with suites either side, and behind each one is its own walled backyard. That was a deliberate trade: no balconies, but outdoor space you can actually use, that nobody overlooks, with the door left open.

It was built to be let by the night rather than converted into rentals afterwards, which is why the storage, the parking and the sound separation between suites work the way they do.

The suites

Two layouts, eight suites.

Every suite has its own kitchen, its own bathroom and its own backyard. The difference between them is how many bedrooms sit behind the living room.

San Nicolas

The half of Aruba most visitors never see.

San Nicolas is the island's second town and its most interesting one — the street murals, the bars that were here long before the hotels, and a coastline that is not lined with resorts.

It is a different holiday from Palm Beach, and deliberately so. Quieter streets, lower prices, and the sense of being somewhere specific rather than somewhere interchangeable.
